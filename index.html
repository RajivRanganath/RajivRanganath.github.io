    01Razorpay AI BuildathonPublic code

    LedgerGuard

    An AI finance assistant that is not allowed to close a case until the numbers prove it.

    97.7%of cases handled correctly, up from 89.4% with rules alone. Zero wrong answers let through.

    The problem

    AI can explain a missing payment in seconds and sound sure while being wrong. In finance, a confident wrong answer costs real money.

    What I built

    • A rules engine that settles every case it can prove on its own, with no AI (54 of 85 test cases).
    • An AI investigator for the rest, which can only suggest an explanation.
    • An "Evidence Gate": 7 checks that re-prove every AI claim from the records before anything is closed.

      02Space trackingPublic code

      Orbit Guard AI

      Tracks space debris from noisy radar readings and warns before two objects get too close.

      +11 to 14points better matching accuracy in heavy noise with the probabilistic method, with every object kept on track.

      The problem

      Radar gives scattered dots with false alarms mixed in. Every few seconds you have to work out which dot belongs to which object, and where each one goes next.

      What I built

      • Orbit prediction that accounts for Earth's shape, air drag and sunlight pressure, checked against physics to about one part in a trillion over 24 hours.
      • Two ways to match readings to objects: a fast simple one, and a probabilistic one built for heavy noise.
      • A live 3D mission view in the browser, fed by a Python server.

      04Final-year projectPublic code

      Sensor AI

      Can a team of AI agents run solar-powered sensors better than a simple rule? I built the test to find out honestly.

      The problem

      Battery-powered sensors must decide when to measure and when to sleep to save energy. Teams of AI agents (multi-agent reinforcement learning) promise smarter teamwork, but do they beat a simple rule?

      What I built

      • Trained three kinds of AI teams and compared them with seven simple rules.
      • Locked the test data and the win criteria before running anything, so I couldn't cherry-pick.
      • Shrank the model to run on small chips (0.25 to 6.8 ms per decision).

      The trained model fits on 15 of 17 common microcontrollers
